\begin{abstract}
Fully synthetic images and video are now produced by thousands of publicly
available generators, and a detector relying on any single cue fails whenever
that cue is absent. We present VidTrust~AI, which fuses a pretrained neural
classifier, provenance metadata (EXIF/XMP/C2PA) and a fast Fourier transform
high-frequency energy ratio under an \emph{availability-aware} rule: a signal
that cannot run is removed from the weighted average and the remaining weights
renormalised, rather than scored as zero. On a 400-image balanced subset of
Community Forensics spanning 50 generators the fused system reaches ROC-AUC
0.9196, rising to 0.9487 under a centre-crop control that equalises processing
history without interpolation, the whole gain being 31 fewer false positives on
real photographs with recall unchanged to four decimal places. We then measure
the operating envelope over eleven degradations applied on top of that control,
and report that AUC is nearly blind to the failure that matters: between clean
input and JPEG quality 30 the fused AUC moves 0.013, while generated images
actually labelled \texttt{AI\_GENERATED} fall from 172 to 95 out of 200. Mild
recompression silently converts detections into abstentions at a frozen
decision band. Repeating three conditions \emph{without} the resolution control
inverts the sign of the measured robustness ($\Delta$AUC $-0.110$ controlled
against $+0.029$ uncontrolled under JPEG~50), so a naive protocol would report
that compression improves the detector; and the frequency signal proves almost
exactly compression-invariant, evidence against rather than for its being a
synthesis cue, since the upsampling artefacts it is meant to measure are the
first thing JPEG destroys. We argue that per-signal logging, ablation, and a
degradation sweep reported at the deployed threshold rather than by AUC alone
should form a minimum reporting standard for fused detectors.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
%==============================================================================

The manipulated media that most needs checking is no longer a face swapped into
an otherwise genuine photograph. It is media that is synthetic in its entirety:
an image or clip that no camera ever recorded. Latent diffusion
models~\cite{rombach2022} and their thousands of community fine-tunes have made
such media cheap, and the detection problem has shifted with it. A face-swap
detector~\cite{rossler2019} needs an original to compare against; a fully
synthetic image has no original, and frequently no photographed face at all.

Detectors for this problem are commonly single-model
classifiers~\cite{wang2020,ojha2023,corvi2023}, which have exactly one way to
be wrong and nothing to check it against. An ensemble of independent signals
can cover that gap, but only if it handles the case that motivates it.
Provenance metadata is the strongest evidence of synthetic origin when present,
and is absent from most media in circulation, because social platforms
re-encode on upload and strip it. A fusion rule that scores a missing signal as
$0.0$ treats ``no evidence'' as ``evidence of authenticity'' and pushes every
stripped file toward a verdict of real.

A second gap is reporting. Detectors are overwhelmingly scored by AUC, which is
threshold-free, but a deployed detector decides at a fixed band, and what a
user experiences is how often it still says ``generated'' on generated input.
Section~\ref{sec:envelope} shows the two diverging widely under ordinary
recompression.

This paper contributes (i)~an availability-aware fusion rule
(Section~\ref{sec:fusion}) that excludes an inoperative signal and renormalises
the remaining weights, mapping total failure to maximum ignorance rather than
to either verdict; (ii)~a two-track protocol with a resolution-normalisation
control (Section~\ref{sec:method}) showing that the standard public set
measures resampling history as well as synthesis; (iii)~an operating-envelope
measurement over eleven degradations (Section~\ref{sec:envelope}), reported as
$\Delta$AUC and as the verdict shift at the frozen deployed band, with a
controlled demonstration that omitting the resolution control inverts the
conclusion; and (iv)~three negative results invisible to unit tests and
aggregate metrics, surfaced only by per-file, per-signal logging: one spectral
(Section~\ref{sec:freq}) and two operational (Section~\ref{sec:findings}).

The design is deliberately free of fitting: the classifier is a frozen,
off-the-shelf model, and the fusion weights and thresholds were fixed by hand
before any measurement, so no figure on the public set is optimised against
that set. That is what lets the evaluation separate detection from dataset
artefacts. Section~\ref{sec:calib} tests the alternative directly: a logistic
meta-classifier fitted under 50-fold leave-one-generator-out cross-validation
reaches ROC-AUC 0.9674 against 0.9487, but by moving weight onto the frequency
cue that Section~\ref{sec:freq} argues is reading content, so we report it and
do not adopt it. Because the public set's confounds are characterised rather
than removed, its absolute figures are an upper bound on field performance.

\section{Related Work}
%==============================================================================

\textbf{Learned detectors.} Wang~et~al.~\cite{wang2020} showed that a
classifier trained on one CNN generator transfers surprisingly well across
architectures, the single-classifier baseline most later work follows.
Corvi~et~al.~\cite{corvi2023} showed that detectors trained on GAN imagery
degrade sharply on diffusion output. Ojha~et~al.~\cite{ojha2023} and
Cozzolino~et~al.~\cite{cozzolino2024} responded with detectors on pretrained
vision-language features, the latter generalising from a handful of images of
one generator, and Tan~et~al.~\cite{tan2024} traced generalisable detection to
the local pixel correlations up-sampling leaves behind. Our classifier is an
off-the-shelf instance of this line; Section~\ref{sec:selection} shows why the
instance matters more than its architecture.

\textbf{Robustness and dataset bias.} Gragnaniello~et~al.~\cite{gragnaniello2021}
found reported robustness substantially overstated once compression and
resizing are applied. Grommelt~et~al.~\cite{grommelt2024} showed that common
detection datasets, GenImage~\cite{zhu2023} among them, carry biases in JPEG
compression and image size that detectors learn, and that removing those biases
substantially improves robustness to compression and cross-generator accuracy.
Rajan~et~al.~\cite{rajan2025} instead build generated training images by
reconstructing real ones through a latent diffusion autoencoder, so the classes
are aligned in everything but the decoder's artefacts. Both act on the training
data; Section~\ref{sec:envelope} is the evaluation-side counterpart, holding
processing history fixed at test time and measuring what an unaligned protocol
does to robustness conclusions.

\textbf{Frequency cues and provenance.} Up-sampling leaves periodic spectral
artefacts, exploited by Durall~et~al.~\cite{durall2020} and
Frank~et~al.~\cite{frank2020}; such cues are strong on the generators they were
characterised on and fragile under compression and rescaling. The C2PA
specification~\cite{c2pa} defines signed credentials that travel inside the
file. Provenance is evidence about a file's history rather than an inference
from its pixels, high-precision and low-recall by construction, and the
literature treats it largely apart from detection; combining the two raises the
availability problem that motivates this paper. Community
Forensics~\cite{park2025} supplies per-image generator attribution, making
per-generator failure analysis possible.

\section{System Design}
%==============================================================================

Fig.~\ref{fig:arch} gives the pipeline. A single HTTP endpoint accepts an image
or a video and returns a verdict, a fused confidence, and the full per-signal
breakdown that produced it.

\begin{figure*}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{fig1_architecture.pdf}
\caption{Pipeline. Three independent signals are computed, then fused over only
those that actually ran. Video is treated as images over time: frames are
sampled at 1\,fps to a 60-frame cap and the classifier scores each one.}
\label{fig:arch}
\end{figure*}

% The weight in each run-in heading uses fixed thin spaces around "=": the
% default \thickmuskip around a relation stretches with justification, which
% rendered "( w = 0.25 )" visibly wider than its neighbours on a loose line.
\textbf{Classifier ($w\,{=}\,0.60$).} A pretrained image classifier scores the
image, or every sampled frame of a video, and its label distribution is
collapsed into $P(\text{machine-generated})$. Generic labels such as
\texttt{LABEL\_0}/\texttt{LABEL\_1} are \emph{not} mapped by guessing an index,
since a wrong guess silently inverts the signal; an unrecognised vocabulary
makes the signal report unavailable. The model throughout is a
SwinV2~\cite{liu2022} classifier
(\hfid{haywoodsloan/ai-image-detector-deploy}, 744\,MB) selected by measurement
in Section~\ref{sec:selection}.

\textbf{Provenance ($w\,{=}\,0.25$).} A generator fingerprint (a known generator name
in decoded EXIF or XMP text, or a C2PA/\allowbreak JUMBF manifest marker) yields 1.0;
genuine camera EXIF (\texttt{Make} and \texttt{Model} present, no generator
string) yields 0.0. A file carrying neither makes the signal report
\emph{unavailable}, the case Section~\ref{sec:fusion} exists to handle.

\textbf{Frequency ($w\,{=}\,0.15$).} The image is converted to greyscale, resized to
$512\times512$, and its DC component removed. The fraction of total 2-D FFT
energy outside a central low-frequency disc of radius $0.03125 \times 512 =
16$\,px is then mapped linearly onto $[0,1]$ over $[0.05, 0.45]$.
\textbf{Both endpoints are unfitted}: they bracket the range over which the
ratio moves at all, and encode no measured boundary between generated and real.

The three consult independent \emph{evidence}, a neural judgement about pixels,
a metadata lookup and a spectral statistic, so a failure mode of one is not
automatically a failure mode of another.

\subsection{Availability-Aware Fusion}
\label{sec:fusion}

Let $S=\{\text{model},\text{metadata},\text{frequency}\}$ with fixed weights
$w_k$, and let $A \subseteq S$ be the subset of signals that successfully
produced a score. The fused confidence is
\begin{equation}
c \;=\; \frac{\sum_{k \in A} s_k w_k}{\sum_{k \in A} w_k},
\qquad
c \;=\; 0.5 \quad\text{if } A = \varnothing .
\label{eq:fuse}
\end{equation}

Equation~\eqref{eq:fuse} renormalises over the available signals rather than
zeroing missing ones, and this is the central design decision. A score of
$0.0$ means ``this looks real''; unavailability means ``no evidence either
way''. Conflating them drifts every metadata-stripped file toward a verdict of
authentic, and stripping metadata is exactly what social platforms do on
upload. With provenance absent, the classifier and frequency weights rescale
from $0.60/0.15$ to $0.80/0.20$. The $A=\varnothing$ case maps to $0.5$,
maximum ignorance, which falls inside the abstention band, so a fully degraded
system declines to answer rather than reporting ``real''; likewise a failed
classifier is not an error response, since \eqref{eq:fuse} renormalises and the
other two signals still answer.

The verdict is a three-way thresholding of $c$:
$c \ge 0.65 \Rightarrow$ \texttt{AI\_GENERATED};
$c \le 0.35 \Rightarrow$ \texttt{LIKELY\_REAL}; otherwise
\texttt{UNCERTAIN}, which is a first-class outcome and is counted separately
from correct and incorrect throughout rather than forced into a confusion
matrix.

\section{Evaluation Methodology}
\label{sec:method}
%==============================================================================

\textbf{Track A (public).} 400 images from Community Forensics~\cite{park2025}
(CC~BY-NC-SA~4.0): 200 generated, spanning 50 distinct generators at 4 images
each, all of the \texttt{LatDiff} family, and 200 real from
FFHQ~\cite{karras2019}.

\textbf{Track B (hand-collected).} 16 files: 13 labelled (12 images and one
video; 4 generated, 9 real) and 3 unlabelled plumbing fixtures excluded from
every count. The labelled files are camera originals with intact EXIF, images
exported from commercial generators, and files deliberately routed through a
messaging platform so their metadata is stripped in transit.

The tracks are never averaged. Public datasets are redistributed re-encoded,
and \textbf{0 of the 400 Track~A images carry any EXIF}, so averaging would let
400 images with a structurally dead provenance signal drown 13 that exercise
it. Track~B's 12 labelled images also selected the classifier
(Section~\ref{sec:selection}), so its results are a functional check, never an
accuracy estimate.

\textbf{Metrics.} Accuracy, precision, recall and $F_1$ are computed over
\emph{decided} files only, with abstentions excluded and reported separately as
coverage: a detector that abstains on half the set and is right on the rest is
not accurate. ROC-AUC is computed over all labelled files including
abstentions, since AUC ranks a continuous score and never consults a threshold;
it is obtained through the rank (Mann--Whitney~$U$) identity with ties
averaged.

\subsection{Resolution Normalisation}
\label{sec:normal}

Every Track~A image is analysed as distributed (\emph{naive}) and after a
control we call \emph{resolution normalisation}. Real images in this set are
natively $1024\times1024$ and generated ones $512\times512$, with no overlap.
Every detector resizes to 512 internally, so naively the real images arrive
downsampled, their high-frequency content destroyed, while the generated ones
arrive untouched, and a frequency statistic can separate the classes by
measuring who was downsampled rather than who was synthesised.

Resizing everything to a common size does \emph{not} fix this: a $1024^2$ image
is still downsampled $2\times$ and a native $512^2$ one is not, so the
operation's effect depends on exactly the variable that differs by class. We
instead take a \textbf{centre crop of $512\times512$ native pixels}, written
losslessly as PNG; nothing is interpolated, so processing history is identical
across classes. The crop does leave framing unequal, a sub-region of a large
image against the whole of a small one, which is a content difference and
reported as such. Normalisation is refused on Track~B, since re-saving
discards the EXIF that track exists to test.

\subsection{Classifier Selection Is a Measurement, Not a Model-Card Decision}
\label{sec:selection}

The classifier was initially an SDXL-specific detector chosen from its model
card. Measured on the 12 labelled hand-collected images it was not merely weak
but \emph{anti-correlated}: mean score 0.438 on generated images against 0.574
on real photographs, scoring genuine iPhone originals at 0.998 ``artificial''
and a commercially generated image at 0.003. No weight and no threshold can
repair a signal that points the wrong way. Five cached candidates were then
scored over the identical files (Table~\ref{tab:models}), and the separation
between best and worst is far larger than any fusion parameter could compensate
for: for a fused detector, the choice of the heaviest component dominates the
choice of how to combine it.

\subsection{AUC Is Nearly Blind to the Failure That Matters}

Between clean input and JPEG quality 30, the fused AUC moves from 0.9487 to
0.9355, a change of 0.013 that a reader would describe as robust. Over the same
interval the generated images the system actually labels
\texttt{AI\_GENERATED} fall from 172 to 95 out of 200, a 45\% loss of
detections. The mechanism is visible in the per-signal dump: the mean
classifier score on generated images falls from 0.862 to 0.485, below the 0.65
threshold, while the mean on real images falls further still, 0.225 to 0.046.
The \emph{ordering} is preserved, which is all AUC measures, but the generated
distribution has slid out of the \texttt{AI\_GENERATED} band into
\texttt{UNCERTAIN}. Precision rises from 0.8152 to 0.9500: the degraded system
is not making more mistakes, it is answering a different, much easier question
and declining the rest.

This is the practical finding. A detector reported only by AUC can appear
robust to a transformation that removes nearly half its detections, and mild
recompression is not an attack; it is what happens to every image passing
through a messaging application. That realistic composite (long edge resampled
to 1080 in either direction, then JPEG~70) lands at 122 detections of 200 with
AUC 0.9623, \emph{higher} than clean. Under noise $\sigma{=}10$ only 75
detections remain. Light degradation fails the other way: noise
$\sigma{=}2$ lifts the mean classifier score on real images from 0.225 to
0.511, flagging 97 of 200, and halving the resolution flags 71.

\subsection{Without the Control, the Conclusion Inverts}

Three conditions were also run on the \emph{uncontrolled} set, degrading the
images as distributed with their native resolutions intact; this arm is a
second result, not a mistake to be avoided. Under JPEG~50 the classifier's AUC
change is $-0.110$ with the control and $+0.029$ without; under the messaging
condition, $-0.036$ with and $+0.004$ without. Both signs flip, so a protocol
degrading images as they come would report that compression and platform
re-encoding \emph{improve} this detector. Under noise $\sigma{=}10$ the sign
agrees but the magnitude is overstated twofold, $-0.150$ against $-0.300$. The
mechanism is the asymmetry of Section~\ref{sec:normal}: noise on a $1024^2$
real image is partly averaged away by the internal resize to 512, while on a
native $512^2$ generated image it is not, so the degradation carries label
information.

% "Points to", not "Reveals": the body below calls content the economical
% explanation and says a content-matched set is needed to confirm it.
\subsection{Compression Invariance of the Frequency Cue Points to Content
Confounding}
\label{sec:freq}

Across all four JPEG levels the frequency signal's AUC spans 0.9098 to 0.9132,
a range of 0.0034 against a clean value of 0.9129. We had predicted the
opposite. If the signal measured the spectral trace of generative
up-sampling~\cite{durall2020,frank2020}, JPEG should erase it, since
quantising high-frequency DCT coefficients is precisely what JPEG does and
precisely where that trace lives; a statistic that survives quantisation of the
band it claims to read is not reading that band's artefacts. The economical
explanation is gross image structure, aligned FFHQ portraits being smooth where
the generated half is detailed illustration, which JPEG preserves. The signal
stays labelled unfitted; if a content-matched set confirms this, setting its
weight to zero is the correct outcome.

\subsection{The Confidence Is Not a Probability}
\label{sec:calib}

The fused $c$ is a weighted average of three incommensurate scores, and
nothing makes it a calibrated posterior; measured, it is not one. Expected
calibration error is 0.1154 on the held-out half, and the empirical rate is not
even monotone in $c$: the 0.7--0.8 bin holds 32 files of which only 31.2\% are
generated, the 0.1--0.2 bin 28 of which 46.4\% are. Platt scaling fitted on
the selection half makes it slightly worse (ECE 0.1363, Brier 0.1241 against
0.1187 raw), as expected when the empirical rate is non-monotone rather than
merely mis-scaled, so $c$ is a ranking score, not a probability to print as a
percentage. Fitting the fusion itself, by logistic regression over the two
available per-signal scores under leave-one-generator-out cross-validation
with 50 folds, reaches out-of-fold AUC 0.9674 against 0.9487, with implied
relative weights 0.26 model and 0.74 frequency. We do not adopt it: reweighting
toward the signal the preceding subsection argues is measuring content fits the
confound, not synthesis.

\section{Two Additional Negative Results From Per-Signal Logging}
\label{sec:findings}
%==============================================================================

Both were invisible to unit tests and aggregate metrics, and both surfaced only
because the harness records every signal's score \emph{and} availability flag
per file, not just the fused verdict.

\textbf{A three-character string inverted the provenance signal.} The generator
signature list contained \texttt{"veo"}, and in a few hundred kilobytes of
compressed image data a three-character case-insensitive byte sequence occurs
by chance with high probability. Provenance consequently reported ``generator
signature found'' at full confidence on \textbf{62 of 200 real photographs}
(31.0\%), firing across the set on 65 real images against 23 generated ones:
the signal meant to prove synthetic origin fired more often on real photographs
than on generated ones, each time adding a quarter of the fused weight toward
\texttt{AI\_GENERATED}, while every unit-level behaviour remained correct. The
fix separates concerns rather than deleting: the full list is matched in
decoded EXIF and XMP text, where a short generator name is a word, and a strict
list of six or more characters plus structural C2PA markers in raw binary.
Verified afterwards on the same 200 real images: 0 false positives.

\textbf{The ensemble once ranked below its lowest-weighted component.} With the
original classifier, the fused ensemble reached AUC 0.7918 naive and 0.8461
normalised while frequency alone, at the smallest weight of 0.15, reached
0.9012 and 0.9129. We reported that rather than reweighting, which on this
dataset would have fitted the content confound. After the classifier
replacement the ensemble dominates every constituent (Table~\ref{tab:headline}),
yet the frequency signal is the same code on the same files with the same AUC
to four decimal places, and the fusion rule and weights are unchanged: only the
classifier moved. An ensemble is not robust to a bad heaviest component, and
an ablation table is what makes that visible. The error structure moved too.
The original classifier never caught six generators and always caught eight,
the signature of a specialist recognising SDXL-family output; the replacement
misses no generator on all four of its images and slips at worst 2 of 4, near
the decision boundary rather than at zero.

%==============================================================================
\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limits}
%==============================================================================

\textbf{Dataset confounds.} Three properties of Track~A inflate its numbers.
(i)~Zero EXIF across all 400 images, so provenance is structurally dead and
every figure in Sections~V and~VI is a two-signal result. (ii)~Every real image
is an FFHQ face while the generated half is varied digital art and photography,
so the classes are partly separable on subject matter, which the centre crop
plausibly intensifies. (iii)~The resolution asymmetry of
Section~\ref{sec:normal}. Together these make Track~A's figures an upper bound,
and are why no fitted parameter here is adopted.

\textbf{Unfitted weights, doubly used Track~B.} $0.60/0.25/0.15$ were chosen by
inspection before any measurement; that they now beat any single signal is
evidence about the classifier, not the weights. Track~B shows each provenance
branch working but not how often it works, and since it also selected the
classifier, its perfect result is not an estimate.

\textbf{Scope.} Real C2PA manifest parsing is not implemented, so the system
reports that a manifest is present but never who signed it. Track~A is images
only, so the frame-sampling path is exercised by one labelled clip and by
plumbing fixtures, and every claim here is an image claim. On an unaccelerated
CPU-only baseline (4-core Intel i5-1135G7), in-process inference with the
744\,MB SwinV2 takes 1.29\,s per Track~A image on average, and a
full-resolution camera original takes 4--6\,s end to end through the API.

%==============================================================================
\section{Conclusion}
%==============================================================================
\balance

We presented a three-signal detector whose fusion rule treats an unavailable
signal as absent evidence rather than as evidence of authenticity, evaluated
under a protocol that keeps dataset artefacts separable from detection. It
reaches ROC-AUC 0.9196 on a 400-image public set spanning 50 generators and
0.9487 under a resolution control, dominating each constituent.

The methodological claims are the ones we would most like carried forward.
First, report a degradation sweep at the deployed threshold, not by AUC alone:
between clean input and JPEG~q30 our AUC moves 0.013 while 45\% of detections
disappear. Second, hold resolution fixed in that sweep, or its conclusions
invert: two of three conditions change the sign of the measured $\Delta$AUC
without the control. Third, log per signal: that exposed a provenance signature
firing at full confidence on 31\% of real photographs while every unit test
passed, and an ensemble ranking below its lowest-weighted component. All three
are cheap, and belong in the minimum reporting standard for fused detectors.
Future work: a content- and resolution-matched evaluation set, the prerequisite
for attributing the frequency signal's discrimination and for fitting the
weights at all; envelopes under adversarial post-processing; real C2PA manifest
parsing; and a labelled video set. Every figure and table is produced by a
script in the project repository, recomputed from one per-file, per-signal dump
without re-running inference.
